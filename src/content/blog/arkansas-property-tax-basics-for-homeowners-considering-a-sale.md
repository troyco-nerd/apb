## Understanding Arkansas Property Tax Cycles and Deadlines

Arkansas property taxes follow a schedule that catches many first-time sellers off guard. The timing of your sale relative to assessment and payment deadlines directly affects how much you'll owe at closing.

### Arrears Billing: Paying for the Previous Year

Arkansas collects property taxes in arrears, meaning you pay for the prior year's taxes in the current year. If you sell your home in June 2024, you're responsible for a portion of 2023 taxes that won't actually be billed until 2024. This creates complexity during closing because the exact tax amount for the current year isn't yet known. Title companies typically estimate based on the previous year's bill, then prorate accordingly.

### Key Dates for Assessment and Payment

In Arkansas, property values are set by county assessors on January 1 each year. Tax statements are dispatched in early spring, and the entire tax amount is due by October 15th each year. Missing this deadline triggers a 10% penalty plus interest. Sellers should verify their payment status before listing to avoid liens that complicate the sale.

### The Role of County Assessors and Collectors

County assessors determine your property's value, while county collectors handle billing and payment processing. If you dispute your assessment, you must file an appeal with the county equalization board between the first Monday in August and the third Monday in August. This narrow window means sellers planning a fall closing should address valuation concerns months in advance.

## How Property Values are Calculated in the Natural State

Your tax bill depends on three factors: market value, assessment ratio, and local millage rates. Understanding each component helps you anticipate costs and explain the tax situation to potential buyers.

### Market Value vs. Assessed Value

County assessors estimate your property's market value based on recent comparable sales, property condition, and location. This isn't necessarily what you'll sell for, but rather what the county believes your property would fetch in an arm's-length transaction. Arkansas law requires assessments to reflect fair market value, though actual assessments often lag behind rapid market changes.

### The 20% Assessment Ratio Rule

Arkansas applies a 20% assessment ratio to residential property. If your home's market value is $200,000, your assessed value is $40,000. This assessed value, not the market value, is what gets multiplied by the millage rate. Commercial and agricultural properties have different ratios, which matter if you're selling a mixed-use property or land.

### Millage Rates and Local Tax Levies

Millage rates vary significantly by location because they combine state, county, city, and school district levies. One mill equals $1 per $1,000 of assessed value. A home with a $40,000 assessed value in a district with a 50-mill rate owes $2,000 annually. Buyers often research millage rates before purchasing, so having this information ready demonstrates transparency and professionalism.

## Impact of Exemptions and Credits on Sale Price

Tax exemptions reduce your annual burden but also affect buyer expectations. Some exemptions transfer automatically, while others require new applications after the sale.

### The Homestead Property Tax Credit

Arkansas offers a homestead tax credit of up to $425 annually for owner-occupied primary residences. You must have owned and occupied the property as of January 1 to qualify for that year's credit. Sellers should note this credit on their disclosure documents since buyers will want to know the property qualifies for this reduction once they establish residency.

### Amendment 79: Assessment Caps and Freezes

Amendment 79 caps annual assessment increases at 5% for homesteads and 10% for non-homesteads, regardless of actual market appreciation. This protection ends upon sale, meaning the buyer's first assessment may jump significantly to reflect the current market value. A home assessed at $150,000 that's actually worth $250,000 will see the new owner's assessment rise substantially. Buyers who understand this can negotiate accordingly.

### Transferability of Exemptions to the Buyer

The homestead credit doesn't automatically transfer. New owners must apply separately after closing and establishing the property as their primary residence. Sellers should clarify this during negotiations so buyers don't assume they'll immediately receive the same tax bill. Providing documentation of your current exemptions helps buyers understand the true cost of ownership.

## Tax Proration and Closing Costs for Sellers

The closing table is where property tax responsibilities are divided between the buyer and the seller. Proper proration prevents disputes and ensures neither party overpays.

### Negotiating Tax Splits at the Closing Table

Standard Arkansas practice prorates taxes based on the closing date. If you close on June 30, you're responsible for approximately half the year's taxes. Since the actual bill won't arrive until the following year, title companies estimate based on the previous year's amount plus any known mileage changes. Sellers can negotiate different proration terms, though most stick with the standard daily calculation.

### Settling Outstanding Liens and Delinquencies

Unpaid property taxes create liens that must be satisfied before title transfers. If you've fallen behind on payments, the title company will deduct those amounts from your proceeds at closing. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When are Arkansas property taxes due each year?

Arkansas property taxes are due in full by October 15 each year. Late payments incur a 10% penalty plus interest charges.

### How does the arrears system affect my home sale?

Since Arkansas taxes are paid in arrears, you'll owe for the previous year even though the bill hasn't arrived yet. Title companies estimate and prorate these costs at closing based on previous year amounts.

### Will my buyer receive the same tax bill I've been paying?

Not necessarily. Amendment 79's assessment caps end upon sale, so the new owner's assessment may increase to reflect the current market value. Buyers should expect potential increases, especially in appreciating markets.
