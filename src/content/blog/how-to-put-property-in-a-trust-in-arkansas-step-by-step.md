If you own property in Arkansas and want to make sure it's handled smoothly after you're gone, putting it in a trust is a smart move. Trusts help avoid [probate](https://www.wapelaw.com/Practice-Areas/Probate-Estate-Administration), keep things private, and give you control over how your property is managed or passed on. But how do you actually put your property in a trust in Arkansas? Let's walk through the process step by step, so you know exactly what to expect.

## What Is a Trust and Why Use One in Arkansas?

A trust is a legal arrangement where you transfer ownership of your property to a trustee, who holds and manages it for the benefit of your chosen beneficiaries. In Arkansas, trusts are a popular way to protect assets, avoid probate court, and ensure your wishes are followed without delays.

There are different types of trusts, but the most common for property owners is a revocable living trust. This means you can change or cancel the trust while you're alive, and it becomes irrevocable after your death. It gives you flexibility and control, plus a smoother transfer of property to heirs.

In addition to revocable living trusts, Arkansas residents may also consider irrevocable trusts, which cannot be altered once established. These trusts can provide significant tax benefits and asset protection, making them an attractive option for individuals looking to safeguard their wealth from creditors or legal claims. For example, if you have a family business or substantial investments, placing these assets in an irrevocable trust can help shield them from potential lawsuits or divorce settlements, ensuring that your beneficiaries receive the full benefit of your hard work.

Moreover, trusts can also serve specific purposes beyond simple asset distribution. Special needs trusts, for instance, are designed to provide for a beneficiary with disabilities without jeopardizing their eligibility for government assistance programs. Similarly, charitable trusts allow you to support your favorite causes while also enjoying tax benefits. By carefully considering the type of trust that aligns with your goals, you can create a tailored [estate plan](https://a.arlawhelp.org/wills-and-estates/introduction) that reflects your values and priorities, ensuring that your legacy endures for generations to come.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Type of Trust

Before you can put your property in a trust, decide which trust fits your needs. A revocable living trust is usually the go-to for homeowners because it's flexible and simple. If you want to protect assets from creditors or reduce estate taxes, you might look into irrevocable trusts, but those are more complex.

It's a good idea to consult with an estate planning attorney in Arkansas to help you pick the right trust type based on your goals and property situation.

## Step 2: Create the Trust Document

The trust document is the legal document that sets up the trust. It names the trustee (often yourself initially), the beneficiaries, and details how the property should be managed or distributed. This document must comply with Arkansas state laws to be valid.

Many people work with a lawyer to draft this document, but there are also reputable online services if your situation is straightforward. Just make sure the trust clearly identifies the property you want to include and spells out your instructions.

## Step 3: Sign the Trust Document with a Notary

Once the trust document is ready, you'll need to sign it in front of a notary public. Arkansas requires notarization to make the trust official and enforceable. This step helps prevent fraud and ensures the document is legally binding.

Keep the original trust document in a safe place, and give copies to your trustee and beneficiaries as needed.

## Step 4: Transfer Your Property into the Trust

This is the crucial step where you actually put your property into the trust. In Arkansas, this usually means changing the title of your property from your name to the name of the trust.

### How to Transfer Property Title

- **Get the deed:** Find the current deed to your property. This is the legal document showing ownership.
- **Prepare a new deed:** You'll need to create a new deed transferring ownership from you to your trust. This is often a quitclaim deed or a warranty deed, depending on your situation.
- **Include the trust name:** The new deed should list the trustee's name, followed by the name of the trust, for example, "John Smith, Trustee of the Smith Family Trust."
- **Sign and notarize:** Sign the new deed in front of a notary public.
- **File with the county:** Submit the new deed to the county recorder's office where the property is located. This officially updates public records.

Without this transfer, the property won't be part of the trust, and your estate may still have to go through probate.

## Step 5: Update Related Documents and Notify Interested Parties

After transferring your property into the trust, review other documents like your homeowner's insurance policy, mortgage, and tax records. You may need to update the trust as the owner on these accounts.

Also, let your family or beneficiaries know about the trust so they understand your plans and where to find the documents.

## Common Questions About Putting Property in a Trust in Arkansas

### Can I put any type of property in a trust?

Yes, real estate, including your home, rental properties, and land, can be placed in a trust. Personal property, like cars or valuable items, can also be included, but real estate is the most common.

### Do I need a lawyer to create a trust in Arkansas?

While it's not legally required, working with an estate planning attorney is highly recommended. They ensure your trust complies with Arkansas laws and fits your specific needs. DIY trusts can work, but mistakes may cause problems later.

### How long does it take to put property in a trust?

Creating the trust document can take a few days to a few weeks, depending on complexity. Transferring the deed usually takes a few days after signing, depending on the county recording office's processing times.

### Will putting property in a trust affect my mortgage?

Generally, transferring property into a trust does not trigger the mortgage due-on-sale clause if you are the original borrower and remain the trustee. It's wise to notify your lender of the change to avoid surprises.

### What happens if I don't transfer the property deed to the trust?

If the deed remains in your name, the property is not part of the trust. That means it will go through probate after your death, which can be time-consuming and costly.
