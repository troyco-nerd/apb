If you're thinking about buying or selling a home in Arkansas, one crucial step is checking whether there's a lien on the property. Liens can complicate a sale, delay closing, or even cause legal headaches down the road. Knowing how to determine whether a lien exists can save you time, money, and stress.

This guide will walk you through simple ways to check for liens on Arkansas properties, explain why they matter, and offer tips on what to do if you find one.

## What Is a Lien and Why Should You Care?

[A lien](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-is-a-lien/) is a legal claim against a property, usually because the owner owes money to someone else. It could be due to [unpaid taxes](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/what-happens-if-i-dont-pay-property-taxes-arkansas.html), an unpaid contractor, a mortgage, or even a court judgment. When a lien exists, it means the property can't be sold or refinanced without first settling the debt.

For sellers, liens can delay the sale or reduce the amount of money they receive. For buyers, purchasing a property with a lien means you might inherit that debt or face legal issues later. That's why checking for liens is a crucial step before making any decisions.

## How to Check for Liens on Property in Arkansas

There are several ways to find out if a property in Arkansas has a lien. The process is straightforward, and you don't need to be a legal expert to do it.

### 1. Visit the County Recorder's Office

Arkansas records liens at the county level, so the first place to check is the county recorder or clerk's office where the property is located. Many counties have online databases where you can search by the property address or the owner's name.

If the county you're interested in doesn't offer online access, you can visit the office in person. They can help you pull up the public records and show you any liens filed against the property.

### 2. Use the Arkansas Online Records Search

Some Arkansas counties provide online portals for property records, including liens. For example, Pulaski County and others have searchable databases. You can enter the property address or the owner's name to see recorded liens, mortgages, or judgments.

Keep in mind that not all counties have online systems, so it's good to know the specific county where the property is located.

### 3. Order a Title Search

If you want a thorough check, consider ordering a title search through a title company or real estate attorney. This service digs deep into public records and uncovers any liens, claims, or other issues tied to the property.

Title searches are common when buying a home because they help ensure the title is clear before closing. While it comes at a cost, it's worth it for peace of mind.

### 4. Check with the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration

For tax liens, you can contact the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration or check their online resources. Unpaid property taxes often result in tax liens that take priority over other claims.

Confirming the tax status of a property is a smart move, whether you're buying or selling.
