Ending a marriage is rarely simple, but understanding the legal process can reduce stress and help you protect your financial future. Arkansas has specific requirements for divorce proceedings and follows equitable distribution principles when dividing marital property. Knowing how to get a divorce in Arkansas and handle property division requires familiarity with residency rules, grounds for dissolution, and the factors courts consider when splitting assets. The process differs significantly depending on whether you and your spouse agree on terms or face contested issues requiring court intervention. Real estate, retirement accounts, and debt allocation often create the most friction during negotiations. This guide breaks down each stage of Arkansas divorce proceedings, from filing your initial complaint through finalizing property settlements. Whether you own a family home, have accumulated retirement savings, or face complex debt questions, you’ll find practical guidance for navigating these challenges effectively.

## **Legal Requirements and Grounds for Divorce in Arkansas**

Arkansas law establishes clear prerequisites before any court will grant a divorce. Meeting these requirements is non-negotiable, and failing to satisfy them will result in case dismissal.

### **Residency and Waiting Period Requirements**

At least one spouse must have lived in Arkansas for 60 days before filing for divorce and must maintain residency for three full months before the final decree is entered. The court where you file must have jurisdiction, meaning you typically file in the county where either spouse resides. After filing, Arkansas imposes a mandatory 60-day waiting period before the court can finalize your divorce. This waiting period applies even in uncontested cases where both parties agree on all terms. The clock starts when you file your complaint, not when your spouse receives notice.

### **Fault vs. No-Fault Grounds**

Arkansas recognizes both fault-based and no-fault divorce grounds. The no-fault option requires showing you’ve lived separately for 18 continuous months without cohabitation. Fault-based grounds include adultery, felony conviction, cruel treatment, habitual drunkenness, and impotence at the time of marriage. Choosing fault-based grounds can affect property division and alimony awards, though proving fault requires evidence and often extends proceedings.

### **Covenant Marriage Limitations**

Couples who entered a [covenant marriage](https://a.arlawhelp.org/divorce-separation-annulment/covenant-marriage) face stricter divorce requirements. Covenant marriages require counseling before divorce and limit grounds to specific circumstances like adultery, abuse, felony conviction, or two-year separation if living apart continuously. Standard no-fault grounds don’t apply to covenant marriages.

## **The Step-by-Step Arkansas Divorce Process**

Understanding procedural requirements helps you avoid delays and ensures your case moves forward efficiently.

### **Filing the Complaint and Serving Notice**

The divorce process begins when one spouse files a Complaint for Divorce with the circuit court. This document outlines the grounds for divorce and requests specific relief regarding property, custody, and support. After filing, you must serve your spouse with copies of all documents. Service can occur through a sheriff, process server, or certified mail with return receipt. Your spouse then has 30 days to file a response.

### **Contested vs. Uncontested Proceedings**

Uncontested divorces occur when both spouses agree on all issues, including property division, debt allocation, custody, and support. These cases typically resolve faster and cost less. Contested divorces require court hearings, discovery processes, and potentially a trial. Property disputes often lead to contested situations, particularly when spouses disagree about the value of assets or what constitutes marital versus separate property.

### **Finalizing the Decree**

Once all issues are resolved, either through agreement or court decision, the judge signs a final decree. This document legally ends your marriage and outlines all terms regarding property division, support obligations, and custody arrangements. The decree becomes binding on both parties.

## **Equitable Distribution: Dividing Marital Property**

Arkansas follows equitable distribution principles, meaning courts divide property fairly, though not necessarily equally. Our detailed breakdown of [Arkansas marital property rules](/blog/divorce-in-arkansas-and-marital-property-rules/) covers how courts classify and value these assets. Several factors influence how judges allocate assets.

### **Distinguishing Marital vs. Separate Assets**

Marital property includes assets acquired during the marriage, regardless of whose name appears on the title. Separate property consists of assets owned before marriage, inheritances received by one spouse, and gifts given specifically to one spouse. Commingling separate assets with marital funds can convert them to marital property. Keeping detailed records of the origins of separate property protects your interests.

### **Factors Influencing Equitable Division**

Courts consider multiple factors when dividing property: length of the marriage, each spouse’s earning capacity, contributions to the acquisition of marital property, and conduct during the marriage. Fault grounds can influence division, with courts potentially awarding a larger share to the innocent spouse. Age, health, and future financial needs also factor into decisions.

### **Valuing Retirement Accounts and Pensions**

Retirement accounts accumulated during marriage are marital property subject to division. Dividing these assets requires a [Qualified Domestic Relations Order](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-qdro-qualified-domestic-relations-order) (QDRO) to avoid tax penalties. Pension valuations often require actuarial analysis to determine present value. 401(k) and IRA accounts are typically easier to value but still require careful documentation of pre-marital contributions.

## **Managing Debts and Financial Obligations**

Property division includes debt allocation, which can significantly impact your post-divorce financial situation.

### **Liability for Joint Credit and Loans**

Joint debts remain both spouses’ responsibility to creditors regardless of what the divorce decree states. If your ex-spouse fails to pay an assigned debt, creditors can pursue you. Refinancing joint obligations into one spouse’s name provides better protection. Mortgage debt on the marital home often creates complications, particularly when one spouse lacks sufficient income to refinance independently.

### **Alimony and Spousal Support Considerations**

Arkansas courts may award alimony based on factors such as the duration of the marriage, each spouse’s financial resources, the standard of living during the marriage, and the time needed for the receiving spouse to become self-supporting. Alimony can be temporary, rehabilitative, or permanent depending on circumstances. Courts retain authority to modify alimony if circumstances change substantially.

## **Protecting Your Interests During Property Settlements**

Taking proactive steps during divorce proceedings helps ensure fair outcomes and protects your financial future.

### **Importance of Financial Disclosure**

Both spouses must fully disclose all assets, debts, income, and expenses. Hidden assets discovered later can result in case reopening and penalties. Gather bank statements, tax returns, property deeds, retirement account statements, and debt records early in the process. Incomplete disclosure by your spouse may require formal discovery requests or forensic accounting.

### **The Role of Mediation in Property Disputes**

Mediation offers a cost-effective alternative to litigation for property disputes. A neutral mediator helps spouses negotiate agreements without court intervention. Mediated settlements typically cost less, resolve faster, and give both parties greater control over the outcome. Many Arkansas courts require mediation attempts before scheduling trial dates.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **How long does a divorce take in Arkansas?**

Uncontested divorces can be finalized in as little as 60 days after filing once the waiting period and residency requirements are satisfied. Contested cases typically take six months to over a year, depending on complexity and court schedules.

### **Can I file for a divorce in Arkansas without a lawyer?**

Yes, you can represent yourself in Arkansas divorce proceedings. Uncontested cases with limited assets are most suitable for self-representation. Complex property issues or contested matters benefit significantly from legal counsel.

### **Does Arkansas require separation before divorce?**

Only for no-fault divorces, which require 18 months of continuous separation without cohabitation. Fault-based grounds don’t require separation periods.

### **How is a house divided in Arkansas divorce?**

Courts consider the home’s equity, each spouse’s contributions, and practical factors like children’s needs. Options include sale, buyout, or deferred sale arrangements.

### **What happens to property acquired after separation?**

Property acquired after separation but before the divorce is finalized may still be considered marital property. Timing and funding sources affect classification.
